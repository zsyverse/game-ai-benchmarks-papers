## Purpose and evidence policy

This pass adds historical coverage rather than re-auditing existing records. It follows `SCOPE.md`: a method must generate evaluated executable rules, complete games, or playable content. **Canonical/scope correction reviewed 2026-09-08:** three recommendations (A, B, D); both Sonancia papers are held on their own evidence. Shared system names are not canonical identities. None was found by repository-wide title/identifier checks before the original pass. The three accepted papers were integrated into the public PCG indexes and dossier on 2026-09-08.

Discovery used author bibliographies, institutional manuscripts, original venue records, Crossref metadata and cited predecessors/follow-ups. Full texts were read for all four original candidate groups, including both Sonancia papers and the ANGELINA journal plus AISB precursor. Abstract-only leads are held separately. `Closed/unverified` below means **no official runnable generator or reproduction package was verified during this check**, not proof that no release ever existed. Historical videos and manuscripts do not establish reproducibility.

## A. Playing with Data: Procedural Generation of Adventures from Open Data

### Dossier-ready evidence

- **Identity:** Gabriella A. B. Barros, Antonios Liapis, Julian Togelius; DiGRA/FDG 2016. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.26503/dl.v2016i1.801), [complete author manuscript](https://antoniosliapis.com/papers/playing_with_data.pdf), [author publication list](https://antoniosliapis.com/publications.php).
- **Scope:** playable adventure content in a fixed adventure client. The subsection **“Setting up clues and creating the story flow”** converts linked-data paths into NPCs, books, locations, clues and **access conditions**: a subsequent object becomes accessible after the preceding clue is read. This creates playable progression and unlock dependencies, not merely renamed people or replaced portraits. Vague/false clues have a 50% probability and introduce dead ends. The conclusion describes a working travel/interaction prototype whose objective is to reach the target person.
- **Method:** select real starting/target people, search DBpedia relationships, map intermediate graph nodes to adventure objects and locations, then generate clues and conditions along the path. The generated artifact is an adventure instantiated in the client, not independent invention of every core mechanic.
- **Evaluation:** **RESULTS / Table 2** reports **851 runs**, using distinct random pairs from a 99-person list derived from Time's 100 important people (American GI excluded). There are **819 error-free runs**, **32 runs with errors**, and **827 playable runs**. These last two success counts differ because some errors are noncatastrophic; do not label 819 the playable count or silently discard the distinction. Error categories are no path 10, search error 9, parsing error 12 and other 1.
- **Table 3:** cities 3.82 ± 1.39; buildings 7.86 ± 2.36; NPCs 5.33 ± 2.32; items 4.14 ± 2.36; real-NPC ratio 74% ± 20%; average path length 8.34 ± 2.45, minimum 3, maximum 13. **Figures 2, 5 and 6** and accompanying examples show Einstein→Thatcher / Monroe→Mandela adventures and actual gameplay screenshots.
- **Negative findings:** erroneous DBpedia relations can produce absurd geography, such as New York City being part of South Africa. Visiting every place and speaking to every NPC can circumvent careful deduction; dead ends remain shallow, and subplots/hidden items are not implemented. Human experience evaluation is future work, so the automated yield is not an enjoyment result.
- **Artifacts:** no paper-specific official generator, client build or experiment package was verified in the inspected paper/publication entry. Use **Closed/unverified**.
- **Canonical decision:** the 2015 [Data Adventures sketch](https://antoniosliapis.com/papers/data_adventures.pdf) is a preliminary predecessor, not another counted record here. The 2016 paper provides the stronger demonstrated/evaluated method. Do not conflate it with *A Rogue Dream*, whose reskinning limitation does not describe this method's condition-controlled progression graph.

## B. DATA Agent

### Dossier-ready evidence

- **Identity:** Michael Cerny Green, Gabriella A. B. Barros, Antonios Liapis, Julian Togelius; FDG 2018. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3235765.3235792), [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02251), [complete HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.02251).
- **Scope:** **§4.1.3 and §4.2** describe generated murder mysteries with suspect claims, factual contradictions, interrogations, keys and locked buildings. The Java generator and Unity3D client (§4) turn the generated graph into interactive mysteries, rather than leaving it as prose or disconnected narrative JSON.
- **Method:** **§4.2.1 Suspect Selection** evolves groups of three suspects using μ+λ search and cascading elitism: population 50, 500 generations, victim–suspect and suspect–suspect linkage rankings, successive removal of each ranking's bottom half, retaining 25% before mutation repopulates. One suspect is randomly marked culprit. **§4.2.2 Path Generation** builds victim-to-suspect paths with a maximum of four edges/five articles and selected entity types, generating the clue/person/item/location structure.
- **Evaluation:** **§5 User Study** has **30 participants**, each playing two generated cases, Albert Einstein and Britney Spears, in randomized order, with logs and questionnaires. **Table 2** reports interaction ratios. This is an exploratory two-case study, not broad statistical coverage of all possible generated mysteries.
- **Negative findings:** **§6** reports low challenge, substantial neutral responses about fun, and straightforward solutions. Open-data problems include nonsensical/trivial facts, mismatched portraits and treating the German Empire as a city. Only 30 game objects were used versus the predecessor's 135, simplifying the studied experiences. Participants knew the AI/open-data origin, creating a possible attribution bias. Willingness to try another mystery is not measured long-term engagement, and most did not want to replay the same mystery.
- **Artifacts:** no DATA Agent-specific official source/build was verified from the full paper's links. The linked MuseumVILLE repository is another project and must not be attributed to DATA Agent. Use **Closed/unverified**.
- **Canonical decision:** substantive follow-up to the 2016 adventure generator: the paper identifies a new story-generation mechanism, suspect evolution and reworked murder/interrogation puzzles plus a user study. Retain separately from the earlier general adventure-path method; arXiv and ACM versions are one record.

## C. Sonancia — separate 2015 and 2016 papers (reviewed 2026-09-08)

### Dossier-ready evidence

- **2015 paper, HOLD under strict playable-output scope:** Phil Lopes, Antonios Liapis, Georgios N. Yannakakis, **Targeting Horror via Level and Soundscape Generation**, AIIDE 2015. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1609/aiide.v11i1.12778), [official PDF](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/12778/12626), [author full text](https://antoniosliapis.com/papers/targeting_horror_via_level_and_soundscape_generation.pdf).
- **2016 independent paper, HOLD for insufficient generator evaluation:** same authors, **Sonancia: A Multi-Faceted Generator for Horror**, CIG 2016. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/CIG.2016.7860445), [author full text](https://antoniosliapis.com/papers/sonancia_a_multi-faceted_generator_for_horror.pdf). These are not preprint/published versions of one paper: the 2016 introduction expressly identifies added lighting and 3D-audio generation features. **Retract the earlier proposed brand-family merge.** Evaluate scope and evidence separately; citing 2015 as methodological background does not transfer its experiments into the 2016 record.
- **2015 method:** evolves room/door topology, starting room, main-quest statue, monsters and sidequest objects toward an authored tension curve and assigns a corresponding soundscape. This supplies designed level structure/objectives, but the paper does not establish completed playable integration; a static render is insufficient under the repository's strict rule.
- **Evaluation:** **2015 Experiments / “Tension Curve Variations,” Figures 4–5** compare four target curves, using 100 independent GA trials of 100 generations. **“Map Size Variations,” Figures 6–7** compare 14×14, 20×20 and 20×24 maps with 100 trials/100 generations under a linear target curve. Results address algorithmic convergence/layouts and modeled tension, not measured player fear.
- **Playability provenance:** the 2015 discussion says Unity integration is in progress. The **2016 abstract and §II** explicitly present a playable first-person 3D survival-horror demonstration: interconnected underground rooms, statue objective, line-of-sight monster chasing, generated sound and lighting. Do not backdate the completed 3D demonstration to the 2015 paper. The 2016 demo adds integration, lighting and 3D audio, but no new human fear study.
- **2026-09-08 full-text recheck:** the 2015 discussion says “Sonancia is currently being imported to the Unity3D game engine”; its Figure 8 caption is **“3D render”**, not gameplay evidence. Hold the 2015 paper unless evidence within that paper establishes directly playable output. The 2016 **§II-A** extends mutations to lights and 3D-audio placements; lights decrease modeled tension and audio increases it. **§II-B** locates audio trigger events at the midpoint of the shortest path between two room doors, or the center of a single-door room, and selects backgrounds from 40 human-ranked recordings. **Figure 1** explicitly pairs a generated 2D level with **gameplay footage of that level**. These are concrete integration/generation additions, not merely a renamed release.
- **2016 evaluation boundary:** the only outcome evidence is the described working playable demonstration and Figure 1's generated example. There is **no numerical generator experiment, comparison, success rate or player study** in this two-page paper; §III leaves extensive user testing to future work. The 40 ranked assets are inputs, not 40 evaluated levels. Final 2026-09-08 decision: HOLD. The playable demo establishes integration, but does not independently evaluate the extended generator; do not combine it with the 2015 experiments to manufacture eligibility.
- **Negative findings:** **2015 Figure 5(c)** contains an inaccessible room; the structural penalty is insufficient to prevent every defect. No evidence supports a universal solvability claim or validated fear-intensity control. Matching a designed tension function is not a psychological result.
- **Artifacts:** the historical download [short link](https://goo.gl/jcXcBu) resolves to `http://www.autogamedesign.eu/?page_id=119`, unavailable during inspection (HTTP 502; HTTPS certificate failure). [Demonstration video](https://youtu.be/dtcV6v5fHHA) and [another video](https://youtu.be/FUhzm6UiudI) are not a checked generator/source package. Use **Closed/unverified; historical demo unavailable at check**.

## D. The ANGELINA Videogame Design System—Part II

### Dossier-ready evidence

- **Identity:** Michael Cook, Simon Colton, Jeremy Gow; online 2016, journal issue September 2017. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TCIAIG.2016.2520305), [institutional record](https://research.gold.ac.uk/id/eprint/18949/), [complete accepted manuscript](https://research.gold.ac.uk/18949/1/COM-Cook2016b.pdf). Use **2016 / 2017**, not an invented independent paper for each date.
- **Scope:** retain for its concrete newsgame/themed-3D generation extensions and evaluated outputs, not simply as a retrospective overview. **§III** describes ANGELINA3 newsgame generation. **§IV, ANGELINA4: Creative Communities**, accepts word/short-phrase themes and uses Unity to create complete runnable 3D games through maps/layouts/rules plus retrieved assets. Premade rule-database entries are integrated into the world to determine mechanics/objectives; this is not a claim of inventing all rules from scratch. The standalone distributable-game design philosophy is stated in the paper.
- **Evaluation:** **§V.A “Game Jams,” Figure 8** reports Ludum Dare entries *To That Sect* and *Stretch Bouquet Point*, ranked overall **500 and 551 of 780** jam-track entries. One entry disclosed AI authorship and the other did not; the paper discusses possible positive attribution bias, so rankings are not evidence of human-level game design or a controlled comparison.
- **§V.C “Curation and Quality”** samples 30 games from each of ANGELINA1, ANGELINA3 and ANGELINA4. Author-assessed curation coefficients are **33%, 60% and 80%**. These are subjective willingness-to-exhibit judgments, **not measured playability rates, independently rated quality, or a benchmark success rate**. The authors could not obtain specific rating data despite requests.
- **Canonical correction, 2026-09-08:** only the accepted manuscript, online 2016 publication and 2017 issue of **this journal paper** are one canonical record. [Automating Game Design In Three Dimensions](https://research.gold.ac.uk/id/eprint/17354/) / [AISB 2014 full text](https://research.gold.ac.uk/17354/1/COM-Cook2014.pdf) is a separate precursor demonstrating *Hit The Bulls-Spy*. It labels the 3D system ANGELINA-5, whereas the journal labels its 3D system ANGELINA4; version numbering establishes neither sameness nor distinctness of papers. The journal cites **Ludus Ex Machina: Building A 3D Game Designer That Competes Alongside Humans** (ICCC 2014) for detail, but its [publisher PDF](https://computationalcreativity.net/iccc2014/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/4.2_Cook.pdf) returned a 406 challenge and was not independently read. **Retract the earlier instruction to merge the entire 3D lineage.** Both 2014 papers remain separately identified, unresolved candidates requiring independent scope/contribution assessment, not absorbed records. The journal qualifies on its own described generator and §V evaluation. Existing 2011 arcade, ACCME and Mechanic Miner records remain distinct earlier contributions.
- **Artifacts:** historical `gamesbyangelina.org` game hosting was unavailable/encountered TLS problems. No official runnable generator or reproduction package for this version was verified; institutional manuscripts are not software artifacts. Use **Closed/unverified**.
- **Interpretive boundary:** the journal consolidates several systems, so the new record must explicitly identify the additional themed 3D generation and its evidence. Do not relabel all earlier ANGELINA methods as newly discovered work or transfer this evaluation to existing 2011/2012 papers.

## Integration summary

**Revised 2026-09-08:** recommend three firm independent papers—Playing with Data (2016), DATA Agent (2018), and ANGELINA Part II (online 2016 / issue 2017); hold **Sonancia: A Multi-Faceted Generator for Horror (2016)** because its playable demo does not independently evaluate the extended generator. Hold the 2015 Sonancia paper for missing demonstrated playable integration; keep the separate ANGELINA 2014 predecessors unresolved, not merged. Canonical identity follows the paper, not the project brand. Keep negative findings attached to the paper that supplies them: 819 error-free ≠ 827 playable; DATA's two-case study is low-challenge; the 2016 Sonancia demo has no numerical experiment or measured fear validation; ANGELINA's 80% subjective curation ≠ 80% playability. The 2015 Sonancia GA experiments and inaccessible-room result stay in its held evidence dossier, not the 2016 evaluation cell.
