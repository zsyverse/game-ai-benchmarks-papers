## Search and decisions

Compared against `SCOPE.md`, the 23 current English records, and `strict-method-benchmark-scope-audit.md`. Queried arXiv for `"interactive world"`, `"game engine"`, `"playable"`, `"Playable Environments"`, `"Neural Game"`, `"generative games"`, and `"game simulation"`; followed references and author links. Google returned an access challenge, so discovery used arXiv's own search. Search results were discovery aids only; evidence below comes from primary papers and author releases.

The principal historical gap is the **2020–2022 learned, directly playable simulator lineage**. The recent gap is **2025–2026 persistent, keyboard-controlled world generation** and benchmarks that explicitly compare these generators. A camera-conditioned clip alone does not qualify. A dynamically generated world that responds to player navigation can qualify without combat or win/loss rules. Game training data alone does not establish that a paper demonstrates a playable output.

### Recommended additions with direct game evidence

1. **GameGAN (2020), arXiv:2005.12126.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2005.12126), [author project](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/gameGAN/), [code](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/GameGAN_code).
   - Actual evidence: abstract says a pressed key generates the next screen; Figure 3 explicitly depicts a human playing the learned official Pac-Man simulator. Project states “All videos and GIFs are taken from people playing with GameGAN.” The neural dynamics engine updates state, memory preserves layout, and learned rendering decodes observations. This is more than renderer-only substitution.
   - Evaluation: Pacman, Pacman-Maze and VizDoom; qualitative rule/event dynamics, simulator-trained policy transfer as a fidelity probe, and the “come-back-home” pixel-change consistency test. Its central contribution is the learned simulator, not the policy.
   - Artifacts: root contains `trainer.py`, `main_parallel.py`, `simulator_model/`, `data/`, and scripts. Read `readme.md` (lower-case filename): it provides VizDoom extraction and training commands; no author pretrained weight or full Pac-Man corpus link. **Partial**. README accidentally links an unrelated project; use the verified NVIDIA page above.

2. **Playable Video Generation / CADDY (2021), arXiv:2101.12195.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2101.12195), [project](https://willi-menapace.github.io/playable-video-generation-website/), [code](https://github.com/willi-menapace/PlayableVideoGeneration).
   - Actual evidence: abstract defines selection of a discrete action “at every time step”; project explicitly provides “Live Demo Breakout” and says users control generated video on the fly. Atari Breakout establishes game scope; BAIR is auxiliary and should not be described as a game.
   - Evaluation: Atari Breakout, Tennis and BAIR; LPIPS/FID/FVD, action-space displacement regression/classification, and user judgments of action consistency.
   - Artifacts: README contains data preparation, interactive-play commands, training and evaluation, and author Google Drive links for pretrained models/data. **Open**: core implementation and meaningful reproduction artifacts are available; downloading/executing the binaries is not a prerequisite for this availability label. Distinct from 2024 `Playable Game Generation` already indexed.

3. **Playable Environments: Video Manipulation in Space and Time (2022), arXiv:2203.01914.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.01914), [project](https://willi-menapace.github.io/playable-environments-website/), [code](https://github.com/willi-menapace/PlayableEnvironments).
   - Actual evidence: introduction says users specify actions to move players and control viewpoints, and that the environment “can be played, akin to a video game.” The paper trains/evaluates on **one hour of two sparring Minecraft players**, Minecraft Camera, and 12 hours of Tennis. An action module predicts scene-state changes; this is not a fixed-state renderer.
   - Evaluation: LPIPS/FID/FVD, action Δ-MSE/Δ-Acc, ADD, missing-detection rate, camera manipulation, and Fleiss' kappa action-consistency user study.
   - Artifacts: verified root includes `play.py`, `train.py`, `train_playable_model.py`, and separate reconstruction/camera/playability evaluators. Follow-up README inspection confirms three-stage training, Minecraft data/model links, ReplayMod collection and Tennis acquisition. **Open**, with external Minecraft/video dependencies. Separate paper from CADDY and Promptable Game Models.

4. **Model as a Game: On Numerical and Spatial Consistency for Generative Games / MaaG (2025), arXiv:2503.21172.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21172).
   - Actual evidence: methods add trainable LogicNet event detection, externally calculated numerical conditions, and an explicit map with location-specific retrieval to action-conditioned DiT gameplay generation. Experiments cover **Traveler, Pong and Pac-Man**. User input changes state and video; the external arithmetic is an internal part of the generator.
   - Evaluation: Action Accuracy (`ActAcc`), Numerical Consistency (`NumCon`, event F-measure), spatial consistency, and inference overhead; appendices describe action and numerical validators. The paper explicitly reports 99% score-rendering alignment with intended digit tokens, a separate diagnostic from game-rule consistency.
   - Artifacts: no official project/repository/data/checkpoint located in the inspected abstract/body links. **Closed, paper only** (absence finding is limited to inspected primary surfaces).

5. **Vid2World (2025), arXiv:2505.14357.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14357), [project](https://knightnemo.github.io/vid2world/), [code](https://github.com/thuml/Vid2World), [CS:GO checkpoint](https://huggingface.co/thuml/Vid2World-CSGO).
   - Actual evidence: method causalizes video diffusion and injects frame-aligned actions; Section 5.2 and Appendix C.5 explicitly evaluate **Counter-Strike: Global Offensive** as action-conditioned game simulation. Project states “unlimited-horizon, stepwise causal rollout.” Policy evaluation in the robotics track does not erase the substantive game-generator track.
   - Evaluation: CS:GO prediction of 12 frames after four context frames (16 total; Appendix C.5); FID/FVD/SSIM/PSNR/LPIPS/DreamSim against DIAMOND; longer autoregressive examples and action-guidance/weight-transfer ablations.
   - Artifacts: README documents training, inference, evaluation, source-dataset preprocessing, CS:GO utilities, and released domain checkpoints. **Open for the documented game track**, subject to external CS:GO dataset/game dependencies; no claim that proprietary game assets are redistributed.

6. **LingBot-World (2026), arXiv:2601.20540.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20540), [code](https://github.com/robbyant/lingbot-world), [base model](https://huggingface.co/robbyant/lingbot-world-base-cam), [fast model](https://huggingface.co/robbyant/lingbot-world-fast).
   - Actual evidence: paper reports streaming interaction with under-one-second latency at 16 FPS/480p on one GPU node (not a single GPU); dedicated game collection synchronizes RGB with native W/A/S/D input and camera parameters. Model rolls out first/third-person generated worlds; the release includes action-to-camera controls and fast autoregressive generation (`run_act2cam_string.sh`, `generate_fast.py`).
   - Evaluation: long-horizon visual consistency and dynamic scene/interaction comparisons, control-conditioned examples, efficiency, and ablations. Avoid presenting a single universal game-completion score: none was established by this inspection.
   - Artifacts: inference scripts and base/fast checkpoint links verified in official README/root. Full training code/corpus not present in inspected release. **Partial**.

7. **ABot-World-0 (2026), arXiv:2607.19191.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19191), [code](https://github.com/amap-cvlab/ABot-World), [model](https://huggingface.co/acvlab/ABot-World-0-5B-LF), [data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/acvlab/ABot-World-Explorer-500h).
   - Actual evidence: paper defines a “frame-synchronous keyboard action interface” for observer roaming and actor-style character motion, paired with persistent reference-character memory; trained from AAA game, simulation and internet trajectories. Introduction explicitly identifies the action → generated state → next action loop. Official README supplies an interactive Gradio client, so this is not merely an offline prescribed-camera video demo.
   - Evaluation: WorldRoamBench action/vision/physics/memory scores, long interaction rollouts, LongForcing ablations, latency/VRAM/throughput. Paper reports up to 16 FPS at 1280×704 and 1.2-second action-to-first-frame latency on one RTX 5090; throughput must not be conflated with input latency.
   - Artifacts: working-source layout, explicit checkpoint download commands, 500-hour and 4D dataset links. **Partial**: inference/checkpoints/data are visible, but full training recipe/corpus equivalence was not established.

8. **ReWorld (2026), arXiv:2608.23565.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23565), [project](https://zhifeichen097.github.io/ReWorld/).
   - Actual evidence: action-conditioned four-step autoregressive streaming of “photorealistic, game-style, and stylized worlds”; source includes 18,387 curated free-roaming clips from 79 games, and controls share a metric movement scale. Its game-world scope is **navigation**, not generation of new combat mechanics. The paper explicitly specifies real-time response and input-driven continuation, not just a one-shot camera-conditioned clip.
   - Evaluation: camera rotation/translation and motion consistency against six models; seven VBench dimensions; palindromic revisits and needle-in-haystack recall up to 64 s / 384 latents; cache/training ablations.
   - Artifacts: inspected project exposes paper and videos without code/model/data links. **Closed**.

### Generator benchmarks: suitable if navigable generated game worlds retain current scope

1. **WorldMark (2026), arXiv:2604.21686.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21686), [project](https://alayalab.github.io/WorldMark/), [code/data](https://github.com/AlayaLab/WorldMark).
   - Formal task: an initial image and shared W/S/A/D/L/R action sequence are mapped through per-model adapters into native controls; ten interactive generators (including Matrix-Game and GameCraft) are compared over 500 standardized cases. This is a generation benchmark, not a score for game-playing agents or a metric-only paper.
   - Protocol/evaluation: 15 action programs, first/third-person and real/stylized domains; per-axis direction accuracy/purity, response latency, motion stability, memory and visual quality. It also evaluates camera-conditioned models, which alone should not be reclassified as game engines.
   - Artifacts: actual root has `arena_inputs/`, `evaluation/`, `generation/`; README defines generated-video directory layout and 20/40/60-second trajectories. **Open** for released inputs/evaluation; third-party generator installation remains external.

2. **WorldRoamBench (2026), arXiv:2606.31672.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31672), [project/leaderboard](https://worldroam.amap.com).
   - Formal task: 600+ generated-world navigation cases, 10–60-second WASD/IJKL programs, first/third-person Nature/Urban/Indoor worlds; comparison includes Matrix-Game 2/3, HY-World 1.5, Yume, LingBot and commercial interactive worlds. Controlled generator comparison and fixed task/protocol meet benchmark-role requirements.
   - Evaluation: per-frame action following, segment-level visual drift, controllability-gated interaction physics, scene/subject memory. The benchmark is world navigation and consistency, not conventional game completion.
   - **Corrected artifact verdict: Partial.** “Download link is not configured yet” is hidden fallback text, not the active download state. The site's JavaScript configures a [live ZIP](https://amap-cvlab.oss-cn-zhangjiakou.aliyuncs.com/worldroambench/worldroam.zip), independently verified by HTTP Range and ZIP-directory parsing: 1,006 action.json/PNG pairs (648 action/vision, 211 memory, 147 physics); no Python evaluator files in this archive. Distinguish the paper's 600+ cases from the larger released input set. The previous Closed conclusion was incorrect.

## Matrix-Game canonical split (supersedes initial merge decision)

[Matrix-Game 3.5, arXiv:2608.29910](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.29910) was submitted 30 August 2026. Independent full-text comparison establishes distinct contributions and experiments in 1.0, 2.0, 3.0 and 3.5: these are now four canonical paper records, not one version-family row. Hunyuan-GameCraft 1/2 and the 2024/2025 GenieRedux papers are likewise separate records. Merely sharing a repository does not merge paper identities.

- Generation: explicit 3D patch retrieval plus tiled projective camera conditioning; separate static/dynamic representation; progressive perceptual-flow/self-rollout distillation for minute-long streaming first/third-person interaction.
- Evaluation actually read: one-minute Simple/Hard trajectories, rotation/translation/CMC error, VBench, same-pose PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS revisits, temporal imaging-quality degradation, memory and throughput.
- [Official code](https://github.com/Riemann-Dynamics/Matrix-Game-3.5), [base weights](https://huggingface.co/RiemannDynamics/Matrix-Game-3.5-Base), [distilled weights](https://huggingface.co/RiemannDynamics/Matrix-Game-3.5-Distilled). Root contains `infer.py`, `infer_distilled.py`, `diffsynth/`, configs/tests/tools; README supplies actual weight filenames. **Partial**; no full training/corpus release established.

## Suggested bilingual public-row text

### Final follow-up decisions

- **WorldPlay: include, Partial.** The full paper explicitly describes instant visual feedback under streaming keyboard/mouse commands, third-person agent control in generated real/stylized worlds and promptable events. These satisfy the existing navigable-world standard without requiring a named original game or a combat/win condition. Training code, downloadable model variants and official interactive demo are visible; the full training corpus is not released. Its v2 (June 2026) human study with 300 cases, 300 custom trajectories and 30 assessors and VBench/MEt3R/control comparisons evaluate the generator.
- **DreamX-World 1.0: include, Partial.** Sections 3–4 describe autoregressive streaming from self-generated history and control inputs, first/third-person game-style worlds, WASD/IJKL actions, revisits and event instruction tuning. This is input-driven world continuation, not only an offline camera path. The author repository releases 5B/5B-Cam inference models; full correspondence to all reported 1.0 experiments is not established.
- **Neural Game Engine: include, Open.** Read the complete [paper PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.10520), including Sections III–VII and references. Its main result is a learned action-conditioned pixel/state/reward simulator; planning is only a downstream application. Evaluates ten GVGAI games with pixel MSE, closest-tile F1 and reward F1, at 100 steps with three repeats (Table II); separately Sokoban is tested for 500 steps with ten repeats on grids up to 100×100 (Table I). The [official README](https://github.com/Bam4d/Neural-Game-Engine) explicitly says users can play pretrained environments with keyboard W/A/S/D/space via `play.py`; it includes training and ten pretrained-model IDs. Important limitations: negative rewards, hidden nonvisual state, stochastic/global transitions; table II reports imperfect performance, so do not claim all ten games were learned perfectly.
