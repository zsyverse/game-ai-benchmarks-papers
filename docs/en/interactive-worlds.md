# Interactive Game World Generation: Papers and Resources

[Home](../../README.md) · [中文](../zh-CN/interactive-worlds.md) · [End-to-end generation](end-to-end.md) · [Automated design & PCG](pcg.md) · [Scope](../../SCOPE.md)

> Research cutoff: **2026-08-31 (Asia/Shanghai)**. This index is deliberately limited to systems that **generate a game or an action-conditioned game world**: observations, video, geometry, or explicit world state that changes with player input. It does not cover work whose main product is a policy that plays or wins a game.

Prompt-to-new-world systems and learned simulators/renderers of existing games are both included, but the distinction is stated per entry. **Open** means official core training and inference code, runnable weights, and meaningful data/evaluation artifacts were verified; **Partial** means a material component is missing; **Closed** means no runnable official checkpoint and core implementation were verified. For papers that only define the field, **Paper-only by design** means there is intentionally no generator to release.

## 3. Field framing and boundary systems (5)

These papers either define how generative game engines should be measured or sit on the boundary between action-controlled simulation and another task. They are separated to avoid presenting them as five additional low-level-action generators.

| Year | Paper/resource | Why it matters to generation | Evaluation/role | Official artifacts/status |
| :---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2025 | [Position: Interactive Generative Video as Next-Generation Game Engine](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17359) | Defines generative-game-engine modules and an L0–L4 maturity roadmap from non-interactive clips to persistent, controllable worlds. | Position/taxonomy paper; no model or leaderboard. | **Paper-only by design** — no generator release is claimed. |
| 2025 | [GameGen-X — Interactive Open-world Game Video Generation](https://openreview.net/forum?id=8VG8tpPZhe) | Combines open-domain text-to-game-video with structured instruction/event continuation, but lacks a universal low-level keyboard interface. | FID/FVD, text-video alignment, OGameEval-Gen/OGameEval-Ins, user preference, and instruction/control success. | **Partial** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00769), [project](https://gamegen-x.github.io/), [repository/data metadata](https://github.com/GameGen-X/GameGen-X); model code/weights and the internal instruction subset are unavailable. |
| 2026 | [From Pixels to States](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14076) | Formalizes the action–state–observation loop and describes a 90+ hour Black Myth: Wukong aligned collection engine; proposes no generator. | Four-axis analysis of control, state dynamics, persistence, and real-time generation. | **Closed** — no official repository, downloadable dataset, weights, or evaluator was linked at the cutoff. |
| 2026 | [Position: Profiling Game Worlds by Transition Complexity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18079) | Introduces a Transition Complexity Profile so datasets/models can report intrinsic branching, hidden interaction/opponent uncertainty, and temporal/spatial demand. | Probe curves and TCP metadata, not a generator ranking. | **Paper-only by design** — [ICML 2026 record](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/67074); no model/evaluator release is claimed. |

## Explicit playing/RL exclusions

The following are deliberately outside this generation-only index even when their learned models predict game observations:

- **DIAMOND — Diffusion for World Modeling: Visual Details Matter in Atari** ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12399), [code](https://github.com/eloialonso/diamond)) centers on training and evaluating an RL agent by Atari-100k return.
- **Dreamer / DreamerV2 / DreamerV3, IRIS, and SimPLe** use predictive models primarily to improve policy learning, sample efficiency, or control return; the evaluated product is the player policy.
- **GameWAM — A World Action Model for Video Games** ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26200)) predicts observations and executable controls, but its core contribution is a closed-loop gameplay/GUI policy evaluated by task success.
- **ActSWM and similar planning models** are excluded when action selection or planning success, rather than a human-controllable generated environment, is the main result.
